NegationInjection caused an 18% accuracy drop, meaning the model was fooled on about 1 in every 6 tested sentences that it originally handled correctly. This attack was also highly stealthy (0.941), so the changed text would usually look normal to a person reviewing it, making this a real business risk rather than a lab-only issue.
MEDIUMMost other attacks had limited impact
The next strongest result was StressTest at a 3% accuracy drop, or roughly 1 in 33 tested sentences flipped. BackTranslation and TextFooler each caused a 2% drop, about 1 in 50 sentences, which is still worth addressing on a frontier model but is far less severe than the negation weakness.
HIGHSeveral attacks were hard to detect
The most concerning attacks were not obviously unnatural: NegationInjection, StressTest, and BackTranslation all had stealth scores above the 0.8 threshold and text-naturalness signals close to normal. In business terms, simple automated screening is unlikely to catch these manipulations before they affect outcomes.
LOWCharacter-level noise is mostly contained
TextBugger and DeepWordBug had little to no measurable damage to accuracy, even though they flipped a small number of cases. This suggests the model is comparatively resilient to typos and character tricks, so remediation effort should not focus there first.
MEDIUMLarge human review burden appeared
The run produced 446 flagged cases for human review, including 265 in the high-priority tier. That is a sizable manual workload and indicates that even a mostly robust model can still create enough uncertain or unstable cases to affect operating cost and review turnaround time.
MEDIUMOne attack showed output instability
BERTAttack was mapped to a transparency and quality-management issue because it showed non-deterministic behavior (different outcomes on effectively the same input), even though headline accuracy impact was low in this run. For leadership, this matters because inconsistent outputs are difficult to explain, audit, and defend with customers or regulators.

✅ Recommended Actions
  1. Harden against negation-based input flips
    This is the standout risk: about 1 in 6 originally correct sentences were flipped by negation changes, and those changes were usually hard to notice. Targeted retraining, rule checks, or an ensemble guard focused on negation will reduce the largest measured business exposure first.
  2. Add pre-decision checks for polarity reversals
    The core failure mode is logical reversal of sentiment through words like 'not' and similar constructions. A lightweight check for sentiment polarity mismatches would directly address the highest-risk attack pattern seen in testing.
  3. Route high-risk phrasing to human review
    There were 265 high-priority flagged cases and a repeated hotspot sentence across attacks, showing some inputs cluster near the model's decision edge. Human review should be reserved for these specific unstable patterns rather than broadly applied to all traffic.
  4. Disclose and monitor output instability
    BERTAttack raised a transparency issue because equivalent inputs may not always produce the same answer. This should be documented in model cards, customer-facing disclosures where relevant, and internal monitoring so the organization can explain and contain inconsistent behavior.
  5. Retest after fixes with same attack suite
    The current weakness is concentrated, not widespread, so remediation should be measurable. Running the same 10-attack suite again will show whether the highest-risk issue was materially reduced without degrading overall performance.
